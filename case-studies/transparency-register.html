Executive Summary

How a compliance officer works a discrepancy case.

Under the EU's anti-money-laundering framework, a bank must compare the beneficial-ownership information it holds on a client against the public Transparency Register, and act on any mismatch. When the two sources diverge, the bank takes on a time-limited obligation: investigate, correct, or report to the authorities, all within a deadline and with a defensible record of why.

The prototype is the workspace where that obligation is met. A compliance officer works a queue of flagged legal entities, opens a case to compare the bank's record and the registry's record side by side, follows the scenario-specific playbook shown in the follow-up tabs, and ends the case with one of three terminal actions (plausible feedback received, escalate to committee, or close case), each recorded for audit.

The one-line thesis

The tool displays each regulatory decision and its consequence on screen, instead of relying on the analyst to remember them.

Where the discrepancy lies, what it triggers, how long is left, and what must be recorded are all shown directly in the interface.

What it is designed to speed up

Discrepancy remediation & reporting.

It replaces a manual, multi-system, memory-dependent investigation of "why don't these two records agree, and what do I do about it" with a single guided flow that keeps a countdown visible.

01
Problem Statement

The obligation to reconcile two records.

The 5th Anti-Money Laundering Directive requires obliged entities, banks among them, to check the beneficial-ownership data in the central register against what they collected through their own due diligence, and to report discrepancies without undue delay. The obligation recurs continually, and getting it wrong has regulatory consequences.

The scale

In one analysis of financial institutions, beneficial-ownership data held by banks matched register data in only about two-thirds of examined cases. That leaves a large, standing population of mismatches to be worked.

Source: Transparency International analysis, reported 2026.

The consequence

Left past its window, an unresolved discrepancy converts into a mandatory report to a government authority. That report is a legally weighted, externally visible event the bank would rather not trigger by oversight.

Shown in the tool as the red "90 days remaining before government reporting" panel.

The difficulty

The right response depends on where the error lives (the bank's files, the registry, or both), and each path has different triggers, owners, and deadlines. This branch logic historically lived in analysts' heads and policy PDFs.

Shown in the tool as the five-way follow-up guidance and the three-way "where is the discrepancy" decision.

Problem, stated as the user feels it

"I have a queue of entities where our records and the register disagree. For each one I have to find exactly what doesn't match, decide whose mistake it is, chase the right person to fix it, and prove I did all of that, before a clock I can't see clearly runs out."

Research Approach

How this was researched.

Fieldwork combined structured interviews with the compliance officers who work the discrepancy queue, shadowed walkthroughs of the current manual comparison process, and a review of the regulatory text governing the reporting obligation. Each persona, pain point, and principle connects to a specific detail of the work: a field that is captured, an action that is offered, a state that is made prominent, a step the workflow requires.

The regulatory obligation, the discrepancy taxonomy, and the match-rate data are grounded in published AML sources, so the problem is described the way this obligation works in practice.

03
Key Findings

Five findings that shaped the design.

These five findings are the spine of the design: every pain point, intervention and principle that follows traces back to one of them.

#FindingHow the tool addresses itImplication
F01 The deadline dominates the risk. A visible countdown to government reporting is the single most weighted element of a case. Full-height red panel on the detail screen; the countdown follows the analyst into every sub-flow as a context bar. Time-to-report is the variable the queue should be organised by.
F02 Fault is directional. A discrepancy can originate at the bank, at the registry, or both, and each origin starts a different downstream process. The "is the issue at the bank or the registry?" radio, each option revealing its own Triggers consequence box. Diagnosis (whose error) must precede action, and the tool has to make the consequence of each choice explicit.
F03 Mismatch is field-level, not entity-level. The "same" owner exists in both sources but disagrees on specific attributes. Two parallel BO tables, Bank (JPB) versus Federal Registry, with a bulleted list naming each non-matching field (DOB, name, nationality, %, ownership role). The comparison must be aligned attribute-by-attribute so the analyst can locate the exact difference.
F04 The next step is scenario-dependent. There are at least five recognised follow-up playbooks, easy to confuse. Five-tab "what follow-up actions are required?" card, each tab a numbered 3-step procedure. Procedural knowledge belongs in the tool at the point of decision, not in a separate manual.
F05 Terminal actions are heavy and irreversible. Escalating or closing carries audit and legal weight. Forced reason + free-text justification, disabled-until-valid buttons, confirmation modals, a destructive-styled permanent close. No case can leave the queue without a recorded, structured rationale.

Personas

Who the tool is for.

Three roles run through the workflow: the compliance officer works each case directly, the client advisor is pulled in when the client needs to be reached, and the advisory committee reviews the cases the officer can't resolve alone.

Primary · Operator
The Compliance Officer
Works the discrepancy queue end to end and owns the clock.
Goal
Resolve or correctly route every case before its reporting deadline.
Context
High case volume; each case a small investigation under time pressure.
Decides

Fear
Missing a deadline, or a closure that can't be defended in an audit.

Header "Compliance Officer" · all action screens

"I don't need the tool to think for me. I need it to stop me forgetting a step or a date."

Secondary · Relationship

Owns the client and is the channel to get data corrected.
Goal
Protect the client relationship while meeting the bank's obligation.
Context
Not in the tool daily; pulled in when the client must be contacted.
Decides

Appears in
"Client Advisor" queue column · "inform the client" steps

"Tell me exactly what's wrong and by when, and I'll handle the conversation."

Oversight · Escalation
The Advisory Committee (BAC)
Reviews the hard cases the officer can't resolve alone.
Goal
Make a defensible call on complex, high-risk or contested cases.
Context
Receives escalations; needs the reason and rationale up front.
Decides
Final disposition where policy or legal exposure is unclear.
Fear
Escalations that arrive without structured justification.
Appears in
"Escalate to BAC" flow · reason + justification fields

"Give us the reason and the evidence in the same place, or we're guessing too."

A fourth role, a compliance team lead monitoring queue health and SLA, sits outside this phase of the design: no dashboard or view for that role exists in the current screens.

05
AS-IS Journey

How the discrepancy gets worked without the tool.

This is how a discrepancy gets worked today, before the tool. The friction below is what the design sets out to remove.

1
Notification
A discrepancy is flagged somewhere upstream

The analyst learns a client's register data no longer matches the bank's file, often via a report or alert detached from any workspace to act in.

Where do I even action this?
2
Assembly
Pull both records from two different systems

The bank's KYC record lives in the system of record; the registry data must be retrieved separately. The analyst gathers both by hand before any comparison can start.

Two tabs, two formats, one guess
3
Comparison
Eyeball the two for what doesn't match

Field-by-field visual comparison of names, dates, nationalities and ownership percentages. It is slow, and a subtle change like 85%→100% is easy to miss.

Did I catch every field?
4
Diagnosis
Decide whose error it is, from memory

Is the fault the bank's, the registry's, or both? The right follow-up depends on it, and the branching rules live in policy documents and experience.

Which playbook applies again?
5
Coordination
Chase the client advisor, track the clock manually

Email the advisor to reach the client; monitor the deadline in a spreadsheet or calendar reminder that lives outside the case.

Hope nothing slips past the date
6
Disposition
Resolve, escalate or report, then reconstruct the audit trail after

Document the rationale retrospectively, hoping the record is complete enough to withstand review.

Can I prove I did this right?

Intervention Catalogue

Source pain → design response → target outcome.

Each intervention is a specific design move, mapped to the pain it answers and the directional outcome it is meant to produce.

RefSource painDesign response (in the prototype)Target outcome (directional)
I-01 P1 Deadline invisible A persistent red countdown panel ("90 days remaining before government reporting", with due date) on the detail screen, restated as a context bar in every sub-flow. Zero cases lost to a missed reporting window; deadline becomes the default sort of the queue.
I-02 P2 Manual comparison Two aligned BO tables, Bank versus Federal Registry, plus a plain-language Discrepancy Details list naming each non-matching field. Faster, more complete mismatch identification; no missed field-level deltas.
I-03 P3 Branch logic tacit A five-tab follow-up playbook and a three-way "where is the discrepancy" decision, each option showing the process it triggers. Consistent decisions across analysts; reduced onboarding time; fewer wrong-path errors.
I-04 P4 Weak audit trail Structured reason + mandatory justification, disabled-until-valid submits, confirmation modals, and an explicitly permanent close. A complete, contemporaneous rationale on every disposition; defensible on review.
I-05 P5 Informal handoffs Named workflow states (Plausible feedback, Escalate to BAC, Close), each with a defined next owner and a confirmation of what happens next. Every handoff routed and recorded; shared visibility of case state.

Design Principles

The rules the design follows.

Principle 01 · derived from F01 / I-01

Make the deadline the loudest thing on the screen.

Risk is time. The countdown is styled as an alert and never leaves the analyst's view. It reappears in every sub-flow as a context bar.

Principle 02 · derived from F02, F04 / I-03

Keep the decision branches in the tool.

Every decision that changes the path is presented as an explicit choice, with its consequence shown before it's made.

Principle 03 · derived from F03 / I-02

Show both sources side by side for comparison.

Conflicting records sit side by side in the same shape, with the specific differences named in plain language above them.

Principle 04 · derived from F05 / I-04

No terminal action without recorded rationale.

Escalate and close are gated behind a structured reason, free-text justification, and a confirmation that previews the outcome.

Principle 05 · derived from F04 / I-03

Show guidance where the decision is made.

The procedure appears at the moment of the decision it governs. The "what do I do now" question is answered in place.

Principle 06 · derived from F05 / I-04

Irreversible should look irreversible.

Permanent actions get destructive styling, an explicit warning strip, and a can't-be-undone confirmation. The friction is proportional to the stakes.

Dependencies

What the design depends on.

Shipping this takes more than the six screens: regulatory sign-off on the reporting logic, integrations with the KYC system and the registry feed, the data rules the comparison depends on, and operating-model changes across the officer, advisor and committee roles. The dependencies below are grouped by type.

REG

Regulatory

  • Discrepancy-reporting duty. The workflow assumes the 5AMLD / GwG §23a obligation to report register mismatches "without undue delay"; the terminal states must map to that duty.
  • Deadline definition. The 90-day window and its start event must be pinned to the governing rule and jurisdiction. It sets the whole risk model.
  • Jurisdictional variance. Register mechanics differ across the EU and Switzerland; "federal registry" and reportable-discrepancy thresholds may not be uniform.
  • Material vs minor. The "Material Discrepancy" status implies a classification rule that determines what is reportable at all.
TECH

Technical

  • KYC system-of-record integration. The bank-side BO list must be pulled live from the CDD platform, not re-keyed.
  • Registry data feed. "Retrieve BO data from registry" assumes a reliable, refreshable connection to the register.
  • Downstream process triggers. Selecting "bank" must actually start a KYC-deficiency process; the "auto-recheck at due date" needs a scheduler.
  • Immutable audit log. Justifications, timestamps and dispositions must be written to a tamper-evident trail.
DATA

Data

  • Entity resolution. Matching a bank BO to a registry BO across name/date variants is non-trivial and underlies the whole comparison.
  • Field-level diff logic. The Discrepancy Details list depends on a rule set defining which field deltas count and how they're described.
  • Registry data quality. If the register is stale or wrong, "discrepancy at registry" will be the dominant path. The model must expect that.
  • History & versioning. Ownership changing 85%→100% implies the tool can compare against a prior known state.
ORG

Organisational

  • Operating model. The officer ↔ advisor ↔ BAC handoffs assume defined roles, SLAs and ownership at each state.
  • BAC intake. Escalations need a committee process that consumes the structured reason + justification.
  • Records retention. Closed cases must persist for the regulator's required period.
  • Team-lead oversight. No queue-health or SLA view exists yet; it is an implied but unbuilt role.
